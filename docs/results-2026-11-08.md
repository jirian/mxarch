# Flattening results, 2026-11-08 (branch `mxint8-reference`)

Protocol for every row: English Wikipedia (`wikimedia/wikipedia` 20231101.en, first
150 articles ≥ 8,000 chars, `data/text/*.source.json`), 64 disjoint windows of 1,024
tokens (65,472 scored tokens), rounding ties away from zero (microxcaling's default),
all GEMM operands quantized unless "weights only". Runs live in
`/work/cs-471/mixedprecisionarchitects/avant-garde/runs/`. Paired-bootstrap SD of
perplexity normalized to FP32 is 0.02–0.2%.

## Emulator cross-check (job 4860058)
Microsoft microxcaling (commit 7bc4195) MXINT8, block 32, vector ops FP32: GPT-2 Small
perplexity 27.709; our `mxint8`: 27.734 (0.09% apart). Its bfloat16 vector-op recipe

## Software-scaled MX GEMMs versus INT8 (job 4865968, `perf/mxgemm/mxgemm.cu`)
One mma.sync s8 GEMM (CTA 128x128x32, 8 warps, cp.async double buffer) in three variants.
MXINT8: k32 IMMA per 32-element block, then `acc += float(p) * (fa * fb)` on CUDA cores.
MX9: micro-exponents applied per CTA tile in shared memory (8-bit floor halving), then two
k16 IMMAs per 32 elements, each scaled by its 16-element exponents. All three match a CPU
reference (INT8 exactly; MX within 3.4e-7 of the summed term magnitudes, float32 rounding).

| Size | INT8 ms (TOPS) | MXINT8 / INT8 time | MX9 / INT8 time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2048³ | 0.060 (287) | 1.51 | 3.40 |
| 4096³ | 0.405 (339) | 1.72 | 3.83 |
| 8192³ | 3.00 (367) | 1.74 | 4.02 |

NVBit at 4096³ (warp instructions): INT8 75.6M (16.8M IMMA.16832); MXINT8 303M (+67.1M each
I2FP, FMUL, FFMA = 12 per IMMA); MX9 621M (33.6M IMMA.16816, 134M each I2FP/FMUL/FFMA,
45M LOP3 + 15M SHF for micro-exponents). Registers per thread 106 / 122 / 138; MX9 fits one
CTA per SM versus two for INT8.

Caveats: the INT8 kernel reaches 367 TOPS, about a quarter of the measured mma.sync ceiling
(~1.39 POPS) and a fifth of H100's dense INT8 peak. A faster INT8 kernel makes the CUDA-core
scaling work relatively larger: at 4 issued warp-instructions per cycle per SM, 12 extra per
IMMA cap software MXINT8 near 0.33 IMMA/cycle/SM (~0.7 POPS) whatever the kernel quality.
Avant-Garde reports 1.66x (single-level) and 1.93x (MX9) over its software baseline.

## H2: decode versus prefill on Qwen3-8B layer shapes (job 4865978, `perf/mxgemm/mxgemm2.cu`)
Same kernels generalized to {16,32,64}x64 tiles with split-K for small M (128x128 above).
All variants verified at three tile/split configurations. Time relative to INT8:

| Layer (N x K) | M=16 MXINT8 | M=16 MX9 | M=4096 MXINT8 | M=4096 MX9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qkv (6144 x 4096) | 1.27 | 2.10 | 1.76 | 4.13 |
| o (4096 x 4096) | 1.25 | 2.01 | 1.82 | 4.26 |
| gate_up (24576 x 4096) | 1.01 | 1.31 | 1.54 | 3.61 |
| down (4096 x 12288) | 1.11 | 1.83 | 1.87 | 4.59 |
| **sum of the four** | **1.08** | **1.57** | **1.67** | **3.96** |

INT8 at M=16 streams weights at 0.88–1.30 TB/s, 38–57% of the measured 2.29 TB/s
streaming-read bandwidth (see below; kuma's H100 is the 96 GB part, 6144-bit HBM3 at
1593 MHz = 2.45 TB/s peak, not the 3.35 TB/s 80 GB SXM). Split-K timings
include the output memset. Overhead grows with M as the kernel moves from memory- to
compute-bound. H2 (<1.1x decode gain) holds only for MXINT8 on the largest layer; MX9's
in-kernel flattening and k16 IMMAs are not hidden by memory traffic even at M=16.

## Decode follow-up: measured roofline and deeper pipelines (job 4865996, `mxgemm3.cu`)
GPU: H100 96 GB (PCI 0x2339), HBM3 1593 MHz x 6144 bit = 2.45 TB/s; streaming read of
4 GiB measures 2.29 TB/s (94%). Decode tiles now stage 64-128 K bytes per cp.async stage
with 3-4 stages (16x64x128 S3, 32x64x64 S4, 64x64x64 S3). Previous tiles rerun in the
same allocation for comparison. All variants verified.

Sum of the four Qwen3-8B linear layers at M=16, time relative to INT8:

| Decode kernel | INT8 µs (% of 2.29 TB/s, per layer) | MXINT8 | MX9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| KB 32, 2 stages | 194.7 (48–56%) | 1.10 | 1.49 |
| deep K, 3–4 stages | 111.7 (67–79%) | **1.26** | **2.14** |

As predicted, a faster INT8 decode kernel exposes more of the software-scaling cost: the
MX kernels speed up less than INT8 does (MXINT8 215→140 µs, MX9 290→239 µs, INT8 195→112 µs).
H2 (<1.1x decode gain from hardware scaling) is rejected for both formats with this
kernel; the remaining INT8 headroom to the roofline (~1.3x) would widen the gap further.

## H1 on Qwen3-8B (job 4866272, `scripts/slurm/qwen3_h100.sbatch`)
Same Wikipedia text, context 2048, 16 windows (32,752 scored tokens), rounding nearest,
FP32 emulation in `venv-qwen` (transformers 4.56.2). The RoPE `inv_freq @ positions`
product stays in FP32 (not a tensor-core operand GEMM). FP32 perplexity 9.571.
Paired window bootstrap (2,000 replicates, `scripts/paired_bootstrap.py`):

| Comparison | Change | 95% CI | vs 0.2% rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| MX9 vs FP32, all GEMMs | −0.28% | [−0.37, −0.18] | — |
| flattened vs MX9, all GEMMs | **+1.06%** | [+0.71, +1.43] | fails (CI above 0.2%) |
| flattened (LM head kept MX9) vs MX9 | +0.36% | [+0.02, +0.70] | inconclusive |
| flattened vs MX9, weights only | +0.10% | [−0.01, +0.21] | passes |

Flattening costs Qwen3-8B about 1% perplexity, far above Avant-Garde's 0.2% but smaller
than GPT-2 Small/Medium. Unlike GPT-2, the loss comes from activations (weights-only
flattening is within the threshold); keeping the LM head non-flattened removes about
two-thirds of it. Non-flattened MX9 scoring below FP32 is reproducible on these windows
but 16 windows is a small sample; more text and other contexts are next.
